In this final chapter, we'll look out over the **frontier that is moving fastest right now**, built on top of everything so far.
None of it belongs to a single company — these are directions being adopted widely, by open and closed models alike.
And you can understand all of it with just the words from the earlier chapters.

## Routing to experts: MoE

In the Transformer chapter we learned that every block has a "feed-forward" part where each word chews on the information.
Running **every word through all of it, every time** is expensive.
So the idea of **MoE (Mixture of Experts)** is to **set up many small experts (mini feed-forward networks) and let each word use only a few of them**.

<MoERouter client:visible lang="en" />

A router (the dispatcher) decides "this word goes to this expert" and puts only the top few to work.
Because there are so many in total, the **knowledge (capacity) is large** — yet each single word uses only a few, so the **computation stays light**.
Picture opening many service counters and assigning each request to the right staff member.

## Handling long text: long context

"I want it to read a whole book" — supporting that kind of **long context** is also a frontier.
The bottleneck is the property we met in the attention chapter: attention gets heavier with the **square** of the length.
Implementations get past this by tweaking how positions are represented (scaling the **RoPE** from the Transformer chapter) and how attention is applied (fine-grained up close, rougher far away, and so on), so more and more systems handle contexts of hundreds of thousands to millions of words.
The **KV cache, FlashAttention, and GQA** from the previous chapter are exactly the foundations that make this long context practical.

## Handling more than text: multimodal

Understanding not just text but images, audio, and video too is what we call **multimodal**.
The core idea is wonderfully elegant.

<Multimodal lang="en" />

Each kind of input is turned by its own dedicated **encoder** into "**the same kind of vector sequence** the language model already works with," and then mixed in among the text tokens.
That way, the Transformer machinery you've already learned can be used **almost as-is**.
In other words: "translate everything into the same language (vectors) first, then think."

## Thinking step by step: stronger reasoning

For hard problems, **working through it step by step** lands the right answer more often than **answering all at once** — just like with people.

<ReasoningSteps lang="en" />

Have the model write out its intermediate thinking (**chain-of-thought**), train that way of thinking, and at answer time **spend more computation** to compare and check several candidate answers.
These tricks for "**taking time to think carefully when answering**" (test-time compute) have become a major pillar of recent gains.
It helps to think of it this way: separate from the "compute spent on training" from the pretraining chapter, we've gained a new lever — "**compute spent at use time**."

## From "how it works" to "how you use it"

That rounds out the inside of the box: from turning words into numbers, through attention, the Transformer, training and tuning, all the way to the frontier of the model itself.
Even the newest techniques here are, once you unravel them, built from **the same parts** you've seen in this book.

But there's one more turn to the story.
So far the reply has always been read by a person.
In the final part we step **outside the box**: how you actually put this clever machine to work — calling it from a program, making it answer in a fixed shape, handing it tools, and looping it through multi-step jobs.

## Summary

- **MoE**: use only some of many experts, getting "large capacity, light computation" at once.
- **Long context**: clever position representations and attention, plus last chapter's efficiency tricks, to handle book-sized inputs.
- **Multimodal**: line images and audio up into the same vector sequence and reuse the Transformer as-is.
- **Stronger reasoning**: think step by step and spend compute when answering (test-time compute).
