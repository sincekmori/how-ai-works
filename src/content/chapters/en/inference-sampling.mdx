Running a trained model to produce answers is called **inference**.
The training story is done; from here on, this is the using story.
How does the model actually write?

## Text is built one word at a time

All the model can do is output probabilities for the next word.
Writing is just that, repeated.

<AutoregLoop lang="en" />

Feed the current text to the model, receive the next-word probabilities, pick one word, and attach it to the end.
Feed the extended text back in.
This eat-your-own-output loop is called **autoregression**.
The "reply growing one word at a time" from the very first chapter — this loop is what it was.

## The craft is in the picking

The crucial part is how a single word gets picked from the probabilities.
What the model hands over is a set of shares — "blue 70%, wide 15%…" — not an answer.
The manner of picking shows in the writing.

- Always take the highest word (**greedy decoding**): safe and steady, but the output is identical every time, and it tends toward monotony or looping phrases.
- Draw by lottery according to the probabilities: varied and natural, but the occasional weird word gets drawn.

So dials are provided to tune the picking.

## Temperature: the adventure dial

The most important dial is **temperature**.
Before the pick, it reshapes the probability hill.

<TemperatureEffect lang="en" />

Turn it down and the hill sharpens, concentrating on the safest word.
Solid, but monotonous.
Turn it up and the hill flattens, letting bold words through.
More varied and creative — and more likely to miss.
High for songwriting and stories, low for arithmetic and factual questions: matched to the task.

## Top-k and top-p: safety valves against dubious candidates

Even with the temperature up for adventure, drawing an obviously wrong word won't do.
Two methods trim the pool the lottery draws from.

- **top-k**: keep only the k highest-probability words as candidates (say the top 40; everything else is cut).
- **top-p** (nucleus sampling): add up probabilities from the top down, and keep only the words needed to reach a total of p (say 90%). The candidate count adapts to the situation on its own — that is its charm.

Both are safety valves against accidentally drawing the impossible.
A common combination: set the boldness with temperature, and fence the ring with top-k or top-p.

These picking schemes (also called **decoding**) belong to no single model — they are shared technique across GPT, Llama, Claude, Gemini, Mistral, Qwen, and nearly every LLM.
